## Decision

The project is pivoting from treating heuristic action scoring plus sampled
rollouts as the prospective final strategy to treating them as an **official-
engine integration baseline, diagnostic instrument, and fail-soft fallback**.
Completed work is retained: the native engine adapter, exact legality checks,
selection contract, timing and decision telemetry, replay capture, deterministic
packaging, compatibility-deck labeling, and benchmark harness remain useful and
must not be rewritten merely to change the planner.

The intended final strategic core is a neural game-theoretic system for an
imperfect-information game:

1. maintain a public-belief state and a calibrated range over opponent private
   states and strategic intentions;
2. map official observations into a validated abstract information-set game;
3. obtain a baseline-safe strategy from a trained neural blueprint;
4. safely re-solve strategically important subgames or endgames using bounded
   opponent counterfactual values;
5. optionally apply a separately measured opponent-model exploitation overlay
   that is constrained around the baseline-safe strategy; and
6. map the resulting abstract distribution back through the existing official
   legality and selection boundary.

This document is a target and sequencing contract. Phase 6K trains a small
full-game neural *candidate* from sampled terminal native branches, initialized
from Phase 6I. Phase 6L supplies an exactly audited external-sampling MCCFR
estimator and tabular convergence reference on the non-official microgame.
Phase 6M connects those traversal and memory contracts to genuine native
descendants only at a handful of immediate-terminal roots; all resulting
advantages are zero. Phase 6N then validates a one-terminal-history

## Target architecture

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    O["Official observation, history, and legal options"] --> B["Public-belief updater"]
    B --> R["Opponent private-state and intent range"]
    B --> G["Validated abstract information-set game"]
    R --> G
    G --> N["Trained neural blueprint strategy"]
    N --> C["Opponent counterfactual-value bounds"]
    G --> S["Safe continual or endgame re-solver"]
    C --> S
    N --> S
    S --> E["Constrained opponent-model exploitation overlay"]
    R --> E
    E --> L["Official action-lifting bridge"]
    L --> V["Existing legality and SelectionContract validation"]
    V --> X["Official cg engine action"]
    X --> T["Telemetry, replay capture, and benchmark evidence"]
```

The safe baseline is the blueprint strategy, or a re-solved strategy whose
opponent counterfactual-value constraints have been validated against that
blueprint. The exploitation model is a separate overlay; it does not alter the
belief updater or define the baseline. If its confidence, calibration, support,
or safety conditions fail, the action distribution reverts to the certified
baseline. A formal safe-exploitation implementation must eventually certify the
post-overlay strategy as well; the present KL and mixture caps are conservative
engineering guards, not that certificate.

### 1. Public-belief state and opponent range

`PublicBeliefState` holds only information common to the acting agent's
information set: public state/history fingerprints, public features, turn and
actor, updater provenance, and a normalized `OpponentRange`. Each
`RangeHypothesis` represents a possible private resource configuration, intent,
and style with a posterior weight and source. Entropy and effective sample size
are exposed for calibration, search gating, and degeneracy detection.

Required future properties:

- update from public observations and actions only;
- never read simulator truth for inference or action choice;
- retain multiple hypotheses and an explicit residual/unknown component;
- validate calibration after later reveals without leaking them backward;
- record updater and dataset fingerprints; and
- support opponent deck/archetype uncertainty as well as within-deck resources.

### 2. Abstract information-set game boundary

`AbstractInformationSetGame` defines information sets, abstract legal actions,
chance outcomes, transitions, and terminal utilities. `OfficialInformationSetBridge`
is the only intended path between native observations and this abstraction.

The abstraction must be tested for:

- legal-action coverage and liftability to official option sequences;
- public-history consistency and information-set equivalence;
- chance probability normalization and no hidden-state leakage;
- terminal utility agreement with official outcomes;
- bounded strategic value error on replayed official states; and
- explicit accounting for merged states/actions and omitted rules.

Until these tests pass, an abstract self-play win rate is not official-engine
performance evidence.

### 3. Neural blueprint strategy

`BlueprintStrategyProvider` returns a complete legal `ActionDistribution` and
opponent counterfactual values for an information set. The neural marker
protocol also exposes public-belief encoding. `BlueprintCheckpoint` binds every
provider to model, abstract-game, and deck fingerprints, training steps, and an
honest trained/untrained flag.

Candidate research families include neural counterfactual-regret minimization
and public-belief planning. Deep CFR demonstrates neural approximation of CFR
components in large imperfect-information games, while ReBeL combines
reinforcement learning and search in a public-belief representation. These are
research starting points, not claims that either transfers unchanged to the
Pokemon rules or competition budget.

The blueprint must earn deployment through held-out metrics such as
exploitability or NashConv on a small exactly solvable validation game, policy
regret, value calibration, official action coverage, and matched-budget native
engine play. Raw self-play win rate alone is insufficient.

### 4. Safe subgame and endgame re-solving

`CounterfactualValueBounds`, `SafeResolveRequest`, `SafeSubgameResolver`, and
`SafetyCertificate` define the boundary for continual resolving. Bounds cover
every opponent range hypothesis and contain its baseline counterfactual value.
A resolver records budget, iterations, expanded nodes, elapsed time, bound
provenance, confidence, tolerance, and measured maximum violation.

The first concrete class, `BlueprintFallbackResolver`, is intentionally a
no-op: it returns the baseline exactly. The future solver must demonstrate that
the opponent is not offered more counterfactual value than the permitted bound,
including approximation error, truncated actions, chance sampling, value-
network error, and time-budget interruption. Endgames are the preferred first
deployment because their abstraction and values can be checked more deeply.

### 5. Baseline-constrained opponent exploitation

`OpponentExploitationOverlay` consumes, but does not create, a certified safe
strategy. `OpponentModelSignal` carries the proposed exploitative distribution,
confidence, effective samples, calibration error, and model fingerprint.
`BaselineConstrainedExploitationOverlay` currently enforces:

- matching action support;
- no new mass on a baseline zero-probability action;
- a confidence/calibration gate;
- a maximum blend and minimum retained baseline fraction;
- a KL-divergence cap; and
- immediate fallback if the re-solve certificate is unsafe.

This keeps exploitation separable and auditable. Formal safety still requires
a bound or robust optimization certificate for the **final mixed strategy**.
Until that exists, this overlay is an experimental, opt-in abstraction and must
not replace the official baseline in a competition archive.

## Relationship to the original six phases and eighteen ideas

The pivot does not discard Phases 1-5. Their state/history schemas, persistent
plan latent, six strategic heads, belief contracts, plan proposals, selective
compute features, replay datasets, and counterfactual audit machinery become
features, auxiliary targets, priors, diagnostics, or data infrastructure for
the blueprint and resolver. The eighteen ideas in `PROJECT_PLAN.md` remain the
research inventory, with these changes in emphasis:

- ideas 1-3 and 17 feed the public-belief range rather than a single sampled
  hidden state;
- ideas 4-9 and 14-15 define representations and auxiliary supervision;
- ideas 10-12 and 16 are reinterpreted through information-set planning rather
  than sampled rollout as the final method; and
- ideas 13 and 18 remain useful only when teacher targets pass game-theoretic,
  counterfactual, and abstraction-fidelity audits.

The heuristic/search agent from the completed integration work remains a
controlled baseline for ablations and a legal fail-soft fallback. It is not the
blueprint and its sampled rollout values are not counterfactual-value bounds.

## Failure gates and non-negotiable fallbacks

- If provenance is ambiguous, classify the result as abstract, not official.
- If the exact deck remains unavailable, keep all Watchtower results labeled as
  compatibility-proxy evidence.
- If the belief updater risks private-state leakage, do not train or evaluate
  the planner on that dataset.
- If hidden-completion particles conflict with public evidence, retain their
  explicit adjustment/OOD counts and exclude them from safety certification
  until calibrated.
- If a blueprint checkpoint is untrained or mismatched to the game/deck
  fingerprint, reject it.
- If counterfactual bounds do not cover every range hypothesis, do not re-solve.
- If a resolver times out or cannot certify safety, return the blueprint
  distribution exactly.
- If the opponent-model overlay cannot certify the final strategy, keep it off
  in production and record only experimental results.
- If the abstract action cannot be lifted to a legal official option sequence,
  fall back through the existing legal selection path and log the failure.
- Never convert local win rate, sampled-rollout gain, or diagnostic strategic
  values into an unverified superhuman or leaderboard claim.
